# Communities

Learning Domain-Driven Design is not just about reading books and studying patterns. It also involves discussing real modeling problems, sharing experiences, challenging assumptions, and learning from people applying DDD in different domains.

The following communities and community-driven initiatives are useful places to continue learning, find resources, participate in discussions, and connect with other practitioners.

## Domain-Driven Design Communities

### [Domain-Driven Design Crew (DDD Crew)](https://github.com/ddd-crew)

An open, community-driven initiative that maintains practical DDD resources, modeling tools, canvases, and reference guides.

Its repositories cover topics such as:

- Strategic and tactical DDD.
- Bounded Context and Aggregate Design.
- Context Mapping.
- EventStorming.
- Collaborative modeling and domain exploration.

Particularly useful repositories include the [DDD Starter Modelling Process](https://github.com/ddd-crew/ddd-starter-modelling-process), [Bounded Context Canvas](https://github.com/ddd-crew/bounded-context-canvas), and [Context Mapping](https://github.com/ddd-crew/context-mapping).

You can also contribute by sharing improvements, reporting issues, and proposing new resources.

### [Virtual DDD](https://virtualddd.com/)

An online community focused on Domain-Driven Design, collaborative modeling, software architecture, and design.

It provides opportunities to learn from practitioners through online sessions, discussions, community events, and shared resources.

Useful for connecting with people applying DDD in different industries and technical environments, particularly when local meetups are unavailable.

### [Domain-Driven Design Community](https://www.dddcommunity.org/)

A community-oriented resource hub covering DDD fundamentals, strategic design, frameworks, architecture, Event Sourcing, CQRS, and related topics.

It also provides pointers to local DDD meetups and community resources.

A useful starting point for discovering different areas of DDD and finding communities beyond your immediate network.

### [DDD/CQRS/ES Community](https://github.com/ddd-cqrs-es/community)

A community resource for people interested in Domain-Driven Design, Command Query Responsibility Segregation, and Event Sourcing.

Useful for discussions and learning around event-based systems, distributed workflows, persistence models, and advanced architectural topics.

## Collaborative Modeling Communities and Resources

### [EventStorming](https://www.eventstorming.com/)

The official EventStorming resource hub, associated with Alberto Brandolini and the wider EventStorming community.

It provides material about EventStorming formats, facilitation, books, articles, presentations, and practical applications of collaborative modeling.

Particularly useful for people interested in Big Picture EventStorming, Process Modeling, and Software Design-level exploration.

### [Domain Storytelling](https://www.domainstorytelling.org/)

A resource hub for Domain Storytelling, a collaborative technique for exploring business processes through stories, actors, work objects, and activities.

Useful for practitioners who want to improve how domain experts and software teams communicate and build a shared understanding of business processes.

## Conferences and Local Communities

### [Domain-Driven Design Europe](https://dddeurope.com/)

A conference focused on software modeling, Domain-Driven Design, architecture, and collaborative design.

It brings together practitioners, speakers, and researchers to share experiences and explore emerging ideas in software design.

Useful for learning from experienced practitioners, discovering new approaches, and participating in the wider DDD community.

### [DDD North America](https://dddna.net/)

A community and event initiative focused on Domain-Driven Design in North America.

It provides opportunities to connect with practitioners, discover events, and participate in discussions around DDD and software design.

### [Domain-Driven Design Barcelona](https://www.dddbcn.org/)

A local DDD community in Barcelona that organizes opportunities for practitioners to connect and exchange knowledge.

Its resources page also provides links to books, community initiatives, and other DDD learning resources.

## Community Resource Collections

These are not necessarily discussion communities, but they are valuable collections maintained or contributed to by practitioners.

### [Awesome Domain-Driven Design](https://github.com/heynickc/awesome-ddd)

A curated collection of DDD-related resources, including articles, books, videos, conferences, user groups, and discussions.

Useful for discovering learning material and communities across different technologies and regions.

### [Free DDD Learning Resources](https://github.com/ddd-crew/free-ddd-learning-resources)

A collection of free learning material covering DDD fundamentals, collaborative modeling, strategic design, tactical design, and case studies.

A particularly useful resource when looking for articles, talks, and practical examples without starting with another book.

## Participating in the Community

Reading and following community resources is useful, but active participation often provides more value.

Consider:

- Asking questions about real modeling challenges rather than only discussing definitions.
- Sharing experiences from applying DDD in existing systems.
- Participating in collaborative modeling sessions and workshops.
- Attending local meetups and conferences.
- Sharing articles, case studies, and lessons learned.
- Contributing improvements to open-source DDD resources.
- Challenging established patterns when their assumptions do not fit the problem.

DDD is a field shaped by practice and continued discussion. Different practitioners may interpret or apply the same concepts differently, and understanding those differences is part of developing sound design judgment.

**The goal is not simply to join a community. It is to become part of the ongoing conversation about how we understand domains and design software around them.**
